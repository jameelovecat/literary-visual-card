# Visual Style Presets

Apply the shared composition rule to every preset: preserve a broad, calm area for typography and keep the focal subject away from it. Use the entire source to establish the overall world, atmosphere, and conflict; use the selected excerpt to establish one visually arresting hook with a clear first-read position. Both levels must remain legible in the artwork. Preserve the semantic core across styles, but do not force all styles to depict the same literal scene. Lock setting, time, objects, spatial relationships, and action only when the user requests fidelity or when those concrete details carry the work's meaning. Otherwise let each preset translate the same tension through its native visual language. Do not contradict the source, beautify away its conflict, illustrate every sentence, or pile up symbols.

## 时光蜡笔

- Core idea: render an adult emotional world through a child’s visual grammar. The scene may be complex and recognizably real, but it must look as if it has been urgently redrawn from memory with wax crayons, charcoal, and dirty fingers—not observed and polished by a trained illustrator.
- Line language: use restless, overlapping scribble loops; blunt wax-crayon strokes; scratchy charcoal; repeated searching contours; doubled outlines; abrupt pressure changes; smudged fingerprints; rubbed-out attempts; accidental tangles; and marks that overshoot their objects. Lines should remain individually visible and dry. Do not clean them into elegant hatching or controlled travel-sketch linework.
- Childlike construction: make proportions emotionally expressive rather than anatomically correct. Hands may be too large, heads slightly oversized, limbs stiff or uneven, objects tilted, and rooms or landscapes flattened into naïve spatial layers. Perspective should feel remembered, not measured. Faces can be simple and vulnerable. The result should carry the seriousness of a child’s drawing without becoming cute, comic, or childish decoration.
- Scene density: the right or lower scene may be richly crowded with ordinary objects, toys, bowls, roots, plants, furniture, clothing, stones, or other source-motivated details. Density is allowed when the text contains a lived environment. Preserve rough paper and unfinished gaps inside the drawing, but do not force a sparse composition when clutter, domestic life, or emotional accumulation belongs to the scene.
- Color language: begin with soot black, charcoal, earth brown, dirty cream, and raw paper. Add a few blunt, emotionally direct wax colors—especially cobalt blue, brick red, ochre, or leaf green—without blended shading. Color should look pressed, rubbed, repeatedly filled, and imperfectly contained by outlines. One simple childlike mark or color shape may carry the emotional center, but do not add unrelated symbols.
- Optical light and shadow: place the finished drawing under a second, unmistakably real photographic light layer. Use a broad near-black shadow field on the left or upper-left for typography, contrasted with warm window light, doorway light, or sunlit paper on the right. Add soft-edged plant, window-frame, body, or household shadows when motivated by the scene, plus restrained cobalt reflection. The optical light must be smoother and more luminous than the crayon marks and must reveal the rough paper relief. For night or dawn, use weak artificial light and cobalt darkness instead of daytime foliage.
- Composition: favor a strong dark-to-light division rather than uniform illumination: roughly one calm dark half for copy and one illuminated hand-drawn world for the subject, with an irregular soft boundary. Let the main figure or decisive object enter from the lower-right, right edge, or bottom as if the viewer has stepped into a private memory. The boundary may swallow parts of the drawing in charcoal darkness.
- Mood: intimate, awkward, bodily, private, slightly unruly, unsentimental, and quietly beginning again.
- Separation from 梵高油画: 时光蜡笔 is dry, scratchy, outline-led, naïvely proportioned, and illuminated from outside the drawing. 梵高油画 is wet, impasto-heavy, color-led, spatially modeled, and constructs light through continuous brushwork. If the image could pass as a mature landscape sketch, polished editorial illustration, or oil painting, the childlike grammar is insufficient and the result must be rejected.
- Avoid: refined academic anatomy; correct one-point perspective; elegant architectural sketching; controlled cross-hatching; delicate colored-pencil realism; watercolor washes; impasto; blended painterly modeling; continuous oil-color coverage; Post-Impressionist brushwork; polished concept art; vector cleanliness; kawaii characters; generic children’s-book illustration; or decorative symbolic clutter.
- Reference discipline: when reference cards are supplied, follow their medium hierarchy closely—naïve crayon construction, dense overlapping dry marks, blunt limited color, dark-left/light-right optical staging, and an intimate entering-the-scene viewpoint. Never copy their exact figures, room, heart, mud, toys, or composition unless those elements belong to the new source.

## 电影诗影

- Base: a plausible real-life scene photographed candidly, not staged as an advertisement.
- Medium: cinematic analog photography, imperfect focus, subtle halation, fine 35mm grain, believable material texture.
- Light: deep-blue reflected light against warm amber illumination; soft silhouettes, projected shadows, or rippled reflections may appear when motivated by the scene.
- Palette: near-black charcoal, deep teal or cobalt, warm amber, and one muted subject color.
- Mood: half-real, half-dreamlike, intimate, restrained, slightly mysterious.
- Avoid: stock-photo polish, glossy cinema stills, fantasy objects, excessive bokeh, theatrical posing.

## 旧梦丝网

- Core idea: a modern, concise graphic poster that happens to be physically screen-printed. Begin with flat design, not with a painting or photograph. Build the composition around one clearly simplified subject drawn from the source.
- Shape language: construct the image from large flat fields, simple geometric cuts, bold negative space, and one cleanly readable silhouette or contour-led object. Use decisive edges and economical lines. Simplify anatomy, terrain, furniture, or architecture into two or three graphic masses; omit nonessential foliage, surface modeling, distant scenery, and illustrative detail. A viewer should understand the subject at thumbnail size.
- Subject rendering: reduce the focal object to a poster symbol, stencil-like silhouette, or economical contour with only the minimum internal divisions needed for recognition. Human figures and body parts require stronger abstraction than small objects: use geometric limbs, cropped silhouettes, simplified joints, and block-like shoes rather than natural anatomy. Remove facial modeling, fingers, muscles, clothing folds, laces, tread, seams, hardware, product-design details, and any convincing photographic specificity. A concrete object may retain a few identifying lines, but it must still belong to the same flat graphic language as the surrounding fields. Use a flat spot-color shadow or localized halftone patch instead of modeled volume.
- Tonal construction: allow only two or three discrete tonal steps. No continuous shading. Use coarse AM halftone selectively on one side, at a cast shadow, along a curved form, or where two flat fields meet. Dot fields may enlarge, merge, break, smear, or disappear toward the edge; they should create graphic rhythm and depth without describing painterly atmosphere.
- Print material: simulate a physically plausible two- or three-spot-color silkscreen or risograph on coarse, absorbent, uncoated pulp paper. Large ink fields may show uneven deposit, dry-pull abrasion, tiny pinholes, exposed paper fibers, clogged-screen patches, bare-paper scars, blunt stencil edges, slight ink spread, imperfect trapping, and subtle registration drift. These defects sit inside otherwise simple flat shapes; texture must not create new brushwork or visual detail.
- Composition: preserve a broad, quiet field on the left for typography. Place one modest focal subject in the lower-right or at the boundary between two large planes. Keep generous negative space. Avoid panoramic scenery and all-over illustration.
- Palette system: use no more than three principal spot colors and keep their proportions visibly unequal. The dominant color should be identifiable at first glance while still allowing aged paper and the secondary color to build depth and spatial rhythm; it must not become a featureless full-frame fill. The dominant field must read as chromatic ink—not black, gray, navy, charcoal, or a near-black substitute. Reserve near-black for small structural marks. Create an obvious warm-cool collision or near-complementary contrast, and concentrate the smallest vivid accent on the concrete subject rather than distributing it decoratively. Select hues from the text's mood; peacock blue is a strong option, not a mandatory signature.
- Palette examples: deep peacock blue, warm ivory, and vermilion orange; saturated cobalt, old-paper yellow, and coral red; dense violet, gray-white, and acid yellow; ink green, warm cream, and signal red; brick red, dirty cream, and deep teal.
- Mood: modern but weathered; concise, cool-headed, tactile, slightly absurd, and emotionally restrained. It may recall an independent culture poster or small-edition print, never nostalgic advertising.
- Hard medium boundary: 旧梦丝网 contains no visible brushstroke, palette-knife mark, impasto ridge, scumble, blended pigment, wet edge, canvas weave, painterly color mixing, atmospheric perspective, naturalistic highlight, or modeled three-dimensional volume. Grain, paper fibers, and halftone are print artifacts—not substitutes for painting. If the scene reads as an oil painting, gouache, watercolor, pastel landscape, or detailed illustration before it reads as flat graphic design, reject it.
- Separation from 梵高油画: 旧梦丝网 is flat, reduced, contour-led, and mechanically printed; 梵高油画 is spatial, brush-led, continuously modeled, and made from moving color. Never combine their marks, even when both styles use strong color.
- Avoid: painterly mountains; individually rendered grass, flowers, rocks, leaves, or clouds; realistic anatomy, shoes, clothing, faces, hands, or product details; fashion-illustration specificity; realistic depth-of-field; soft atmospheric gradients; nuanced light modeling; short directional brush marks; uniform digital noise; a generic grain filter; halftone covering every surface; sterile vector cleanliness; evenly balanced colors; a featureless dominant-color flood; commercial retro advertising; decorative nostalgia; cute mascots; multiple symbols; or literal illustration of every sentence.
- Reference discipline: when a reference is supplied, borrow its flat-field hierarchy, economical geometry, isolated contour-led object, limited spot colors, asymmetric halftone, and worn paper/ink behavior. Do not copy its cockroach, room corner, circles, palette, typography, or exact layout unless required by the new source.

## 梵高油画

- Base: one emotionally charged natural scene in which landscape, light, and weather carry the text's inner movement. Translate the passage into a place or physical pause rather than illustrating every sentence.
- Medium: original expressive post-impressionist oil painting with visible rhythmic impasto, directional brushwork, simplified forms, and emotionally intensified but coherent natural color. Use only broad visual qualities associated with Van Gogh; never reproduce or closely imitate the composition or signature motifs of a specific painting.
- Composition: keep the human figure or concrete subject small and place it in the lower-right or right half. Reserve the upper-left and middle-left for typography. Make brushwork in the text zone roughly 20 percent calmer, broader, and lower-contrast than the focal area while retaining a continuous painted surface.
- Light and palette: favor luminous daylight or dusk and a text-responsive natural palette. Blue-and-gold tension is a useful default for bright melancholy, but not mandatory. Keep the image alive rather than gloomy; avoid indiscriminate high saturation. For interiors or enclosed spaces, never translate melancholy into overall darkness: establish one broad luminous wall, floor, doorway, or window-light field; keep most of the canvas in readable middle or high values; and confine the deepest shadows to less than roughly one third of the image.
- Mood: bright melancholy, restless vitality, tenderness, solitude, fatigue without defeat, and the feeling of beginning again.
- Avoid: copies of known paintings; recognizable quotations such as a swirling starry night, iconic cypresses, sunflowers, or crows; decorative imitation; generic fantasy scenery; heroic mountain spectacle; muddy or predominantly dark interiors; photorealism; flat digital filters; dense brushwork directly behind the text.
